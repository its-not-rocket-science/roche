\begin{abstract}
Reference optimisation resolves the main post-hoc certification
bottleneck for diagonal SSMs; contour barriers are useful soft
margin-preserving training penalties; but only constrained
parameterisation provides hard stability guarantees under extreme
gradient pressure.

Post-hoc Rouch\'e certificates introduced in Paper~1 achieve high
false-negative rates with naive references.  We develop a gradient-based
algorithm that optimises the diagonal reference $A_0$ to maximise the
sampled resolvent margin, achieving 100\% certification on diagonal and
trained-SSM families (up from $\leq 36\%$ with random search).  Diagonal
references remain insufficient for non-normal and Jordan-near-boundary
matrices regardless of optimisation quality; these structures motivate a
diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR) extension presented here as an exploratory
baseline.

We embed the optimised reference into three training-time stability
regularisers---spectral penalty, Lyapunov per-mode penalty, and a
differentiable contour barrier---and compare them on autoregressive tasks.
In the standard constrained regime all three achieve 100\% post-hoc
certification.  With margin $\tau=0.1$, spectral and Lyapunov penalties
are inactive during training (models naturally maintain $\varrho<0.9$),
producing results identical to the unregularised baseline.  The contour
barrier is the only soft penalty that does not collapse to the unregularised
baseline, producing a modest but consistent $\varrho$ reduction ($\Delta\varrho
\approx 0.03$) on long-memory tasks via resolvent-geometry penalties that
remain active below the spectral-radius threshold.

In an adversarial unconstrained regime (lr=0.1, near-boundary init
$\varrho=0.96$), 90\% of unregularised runs overshoot the unit circle.
Hinge regularisers reduce but cannot eliminate instability (10--20\%
stable): at lr=0.1 the hinge is already active ($\varrho_{\rm init}=0.96>0.9$)
but its gradient is overwhelmed by the task gradient and Adam momentum,
so a single step can carry eigenvalues past the unit circle.  An ablation
(Experiment~3b) shows that a softplus hinge offers no meaningful improvement,
while a post-step radius-projection clamp is substantially more effective by
enforcing the radius bound every step.  A sigmoid-parameterised
constrained trainer (spectral radius $< 0.98$ by construction) achieves
100\% stability on every seed, confirming that soft penalties are
complementary to---not a replacement for---hard architectural constraints.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The companion paper established a framework of Rouch\'e-type contour
certificates for Schur stability of learned transition matrices.  The
central empirical finding was that both determinant and resolvent
certificates achieve less than 3\% certification rates when paired with
scalar or random-diagonal reference matrices, even for matrices the
certificates could in principle certify.  The bottleneck is the
\emph{reference matrix} $A_0$: a poorly chosen reference inflates the
perturbation $\norm{A_0 - A}$ relative to the resolvent margin,
rendering the certificate vacuous.

Section~\ref{sec:refopt} develops a gradient-based algorithm that maximises
the sampled minimum resolvent margin $\hat{m}(A, A_0)$ w.r.t.\ diagonal
$A_0$ via a differentiable softmin and PyTorch autograd; for diagonal $A$
the margin reduces to $O(Kn)$ elementwise arithmetic, whereas general $A$
requires an $O(Kn^2)$ SVD path.  An exploratory diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR)
extension for non-normal matrices is described in Appendix~\ref{app:dlr};
unit tests confirm stability of the returned $A_0$, nonzero low-rank
movement on DLR inputs, and margin improvement over scalar/diagonal
baselines in small controlled cases, though systematic evaluation on
non-normal and Jordan families is deferred.  Three training-time stability
regularisers---spectral penalty, Lyapunov per-mode penalty, and a contour
barrier---are compared in Section~\ref{sec:regularisers}; the contour barrier
preserves larger spectral radii without achieving lower task loss or higher
resolvent margins.  Section~\ref{sec:exp3} shows that relu-based hinge
regularisers cannot prevent unit-disk escape under adversarial gradient
pressure: at $\mathrm{lr}=0.1$ with near-boundary initialisation
($\varrho_{\rm init}=0.96>0.9$), the hinge is active but its gradient is
overwhelmed by the task gradient, so a single Adam step overshoots.  Section~\ref{sec:exp3b}
ablates two alternatives: a softplus hinge (non-zero gradient everywhere) and
a post-step radius projection (hard-ish clamp every step).  A
sigmoid-constrained model enforcing $\varrho < 0.98$ by construction achieves
$100\%$ stability on every seed.

\paragraph{Certificate in brief.}
The certificate studied here is a sufficient condition for Schur
stability: it compares a learned transition matrix $A$ to a stable
reference $A_0$ on the unit circle.  If the sampled resolvent margin
$\hat{m}(A,A_0)=\min_{k}\bigl(1-\|(z_k I-A_0)^{-1}(A_0-A)\|\bigr)$ is
positive at all $K$ sample points, then by Rouch\'e's theorem $A$ is
certified Schur stable.  The key empirical difficulty is not evaluating
the certificate but choosing a reference $A_0$ that keeps $\|A_0-A\|$
small relative to the resolvent margin of $A_0$.

\paragraph{Relation to companion paper.}
All certificate definitions, Theorems 1--4, and the companion codebase
(\texttt{src/roche/}) remain unchanged.  This paper adds gradient
reference optimisation (\texttt{reference\_opt.py}), three regulariser
implementations (\texttt{regularisers.py}), and an Adam-based SSM
trainer in \texttt{ssm.py}.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Diagonal SSM architectures beyond S4~\cite{gu2022s4} and
LRU~\cite{smith2023simplified} include DSS~\cite{gupta2022dss} and
S4D~\cite{gu2022s4d}, which show that diagonal parameterisations match
the sequence-modelling performance of full structured state spaces.
Hard architectural stability constraints have a long history in
recurrent networks: unitary RNNs~\cite{arjovsky2016unitary} enforce
$\varrho(A)=1$ exactly via unitary parameterisation, and
Lie-group-constrained networks~\cite{lezcano2019cheap} maintain
stability on matrix manifolds during training.
Training-time stability regularisation for recurrent
models is studied in~\cite{miller2019stable,erichson2021lipschitz};
Experiment~2 here directly compares three regulariser families on
diagonal SSMs, and Experiment~3 establishes their limits under
adversarial gradient pressure.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Contour certificates (recap)}

Let $A \in \C^{n \times n}$ and $A_0 \in \C^{n \times n}$ be Schur stable.
The \emph{resolvent margin} at $z = e^{i\theta}$ is
\[
  m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, z)
  = 1 - \norm{(zI - A_0)^{-1}(A_0 - A)}_2.
\]
When $\min_\theta m_{\mathrm{res}} > 0$, Rouch\'e's theorem guarantees
$\varrho(A) < 1$.

\begin{theorem}[Resolvent certificate; companion paper, Thm.~2--3]
\label{thm:res_cert}
Let $A_0$ be Schur stable.  If
$\min_\theta m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, e^{i\theta}) > 0$, then
$\varrho(A) < 1$.  A finite-grid version converts a sampled positive
margin $\hat{m}(A, A_0) = \min_k m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, z_k) > 0$
over $K$ equispaced contour points into a continuum guarantee via a
Lipschitz continuity argument on the resolvent.
\end{theorem}

\noindent This paper takes Theorem~\ref{thm:res_cert} as given and
focuses on optimising $A_0$ to make $\hat{m}$ as large as possible.

\subsection{The reference selection problem}

Define the \emph{optimal diagonal reference} as
\[
  A_0^* = \argmax_{A_0 \in \mathcal{D}} \min_{\theta} m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, \theta),
\]
where $\mathcal{D}$ is the set of diagonal Schur-stable matrices.
Random search over 50--200 diagonal candidates (companion paper)
achieves near-zero certification rates for normal and non-normal
families, motivating a gradient-based approach.

\subsection{Diagonal fast path}

For $A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_n)$ with $A$ arbitrary,
\[
  (zI - A_0)^{-1}(A_0 - A) = \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{z - d_i}\right)(A_0 - A),
\]
i.e.\ each row $i$ of $A_0 - A$ is scaled by $1/(z - d_i)$.  The
2-norm of this product equals the largest singular value of a
row-scaled matrix, which costs $O(Kn^2)$ in general.  When $A$ is
\emph{also diagonal} (as in diagonal complex SSMs such as
S4~\cite{gu2022s4}, DSS~\cite{gupta2022dss}, S4D~\cite{gu2022s4d},
and LRU~\cite{smith2023simplified}), $A_0 - A$ is
diagonal and the 2-norm reduces to $\max_i \abs{(d_{0,i} -
a_i)/(z - d_{0,i})}$, costing only $O(Kn)$ with no SVD.

\section{Gradient Reference Optimisation}
\label{sec:refopt}

\subsection{Parameterisation}

We parameterise $A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(r_i e^{i\phi_i})$ where
\[
  r_i = \sigma(\ell_i)(1 - \varepsilon), \quad \varepsilon = 0.02,
\]
with $\sigma$ the logistic function and $\ell_i, \phi_i \in \R$ the
free parameters.  This ensures $r_i \in (0, 1 - \varepsilon)$
without any projection step.

\subsection{Objective}

We maximise a differentiable lower bound on $\min_k m_{\mathrm{res}}(z_k)$
over a grid of $K$ equispaced contour points via a softmin:
\[
  \hat{m}_\beta(A, A_0)
  = -\frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_k \exp(-\beta \cdot m_{\mathrm{res}}(z_k)),
  \quad \beta = 20.
\]
Gradient ascent on $\hat{m}_\beta$ with respect to $(\ell_i, \phi_i)$
is performed with Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} (lr = 0.05, 300 steps)
using PyTorch autograd~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}.
Initialisation uses the eig-shrunk reference (Paper~1), which provides
a warm start with non-trivial margin for trained SSMs.

\paragraph{Non-diagonal extension.}
A diagonal $A_0$ cannot closely approximate matrices with significant
off-diagonal structure.  Appendix~\ref{app:dlr} describes a
\emph{diagonal-plus-low-rank} (DLR) parameterisation
$A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(d_0) + UV^H$ that adds expressivity at $O(Kn^3)$
cost per optimisation step.  Unit tests confirm correctness; systematic
evaluation on non-normal and Jordan families is deferred to future work.

\section{Stability Regularisers}
\label{sec:regularisers}

All three regularisers take the diagonal eigenvalue vector
$\mathbf{a} = (a_1, \ldots, a_n) \in \C^n$ and return a real scalar.

\paragraph{Spectral penalty.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathbf{a}) = \mathrm{relu}\!\left(\max_i \abs{a_i} - (1 - \tau)\right),
\]
where $\tau$ is a stability margin.  This is a hinge on the spectral
radius; only the largest mode is penalised.  \texttt{relu} rather than
\texttt{softplus} is used so the penalty is \emph{exactly zero} for
clearly stable systems, avoiding a constant background term.

\paragraph{Lyapunov penalty.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{Lyap}}(\mathbf{a}) = \sum_i \mathrm{relu}\!\left(\abs{a_i}^2 - (1 - \tau)\right).
\]
For diagonal $A$ with $P = I$ the discrete Lyapunov
condition~\cite{miller2019stable} $A^\top P A - P \prec 0$
reduces to $\abs{a_i}^2 < 1$ per mode.  The sum formulation penalises
\emph{all} near-unstable modes jointly, yielding different gradient dynamics
from the spectral hinge.

\paragraph{Contour barrier.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ctour}}(\mathbf{a}) = \mathrm{relu}\!\left(-\hat{m}_\beta(A, A_0^*) + \tau\right),
\]
where $A_0^*$ is the gradient-optimised reference computed once from
the initial model and frozen throughout training.  We freeze $A_0^*$ to
isolate the effect of the barrier as a regulariser on $A$: jointly
optimising $A$ and $A_0$ can mask instability by allowing the reference
to chase the model, defeating the certificate.  The barrier fires only
when the resolvent margin falls below $\tau$, providing a
certificate-geometry-aware penalty signal.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We intentionally use controlled AR processes rather than broad sequence
benchmarks (e.g.\ LRA, sequential MNIST) because the ground-truth poles
determine the required memory scale.  This makes the task-loss--spectral-radius
tradeoff interpretable: overly aggressive stability regularisation suppresses
modes needed for long-memory prediction, and the effect is directly visible
in the model's spectral radius.  Evaluating regularisers on tasks where
the connection between stability and performance is unknown would obscure
the mechanism under study.

We compare all four reference methods (scalar, eig-shrunk, random-search,
gradient-optimised) across four matrix families:
\texttt{diagonal\_stable} (random diagonal, $\varrho \in [0.7, 0.95]$);
\texttt{nonnormal\_stable} ($\varrho = 0.85$, departure~20);
\texttt{jordan\_near\_boundary} ($\varrho = 0.95$, superdiag~1.0);
\texttt{trained\_ssm} (diagonal SSM trained on AR(4) long-memory).
$M = 50$ instances per family, $n = 8$, $K = 256$ contour points.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Regulariser comparison}

We train diagonal complex SSMs ($n = 8$, Adam, 500 epochs, lr = 0.001)
on three AR tasks under four regularisation regimes (weight $\lambda = 0.1$):
no regulariser, spectral penalty, Lyapunov penalty, and contour barrier.
Five random seeds per configuration (60 runs total).
Post-hoc certification uses the eig-shrunk reference (Paper~1 Exp~3 protocol).

Tasks: AR(2) short-memory ($\varrho_{\max} = 0.70$);
AR(4) long-memory ($\varrho_{\max} = 0.92$);
AR(4) hard ($\varrho_{\max} = 0.97$).

\noindent\textbf{Threshold choice.}
We use $\tau = 0.1$ (firing threshold $\varrho = 0.9$) as a deliberately
non-trivial stability buffer.  Smaller $\tau$ values make spectral and
Lyapunov penalties fire less often in benign training; larger values are
more aggressive and increasingly suppress the long-memory modes needed for
AR prediction.  As Experiment~3 shows, adjusting $\tau$ alone does not
provide hard stability: a single Adam step at lr = 0.1 can still cross the
unit circle regardless of where the penalty threshold is placed.

arises because the contour barrier penalises resolvent-margin shortfall
directly rather than spectral radius, making it sensitive to the boundary
geometry even when $\varrho$ is comfortably below the threshold.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Adversarial unconstrained regime}
\label{sec:exp3}

To isolate whether hinge regularisers can \emph{prevent} instability
(rather than merely coexist with it in an already-stable regime), we
train with \emph{unconstrained} eigenvalue radii, a high learning rate
(lr = 0.1), adversarial near-boundary initialisation ($\varrho_{\mathrm{init}} = 0.96$),
and a long-memory AR(4) task ($\varrho_{\max} \approx 0.97$, seq.\ length 2000).
Regulariser threshold is $\tau = 0.1$ (fires at $\varrho = 0.9$) with weight
$\lambda = 1.0$.  Ten seeds per method; $n = 8$, 300 epochs.

\paragraph{Constrained-parameterisation baseline.}
As a hard-stability reference we include a fifth method,
\emph{constrained\_schur}, which replaces the exponential radius
parameterisation with $r_i = \sigma(\ell_i)(1-\varepsilon)$
($\varepsilon = 0.02$; $\sigma$ the logistic function).  This enforces
$\varrho(A) < 0.98$ by construction: no regulariser, no projection---the
architecture itself is the constraint.  With the same high learning rate
and near-boundary initialisation, the sigmoid parameterisation cannot
overshoot the unit circle regardless of gradient magnitude.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.}
At lr = 0.1, Adam momentum can carry eigenvalue radii by
$\Delta\varrho \approx 0.15$ per step.
Starting from $\varrho_0 = 0.96$, a single step can cross $\varrho = 1$.
The hinge threshold is $\varrho = 0.9$, so the regulariser is
\emph{already active} at initialisation ($0.96 > 0.9$); the failure is
not delayed activation but \emph{gradient dominance}: the regulariser
gradient is too small relative to the task gradient and Adam momentum.
Experiment~3b directly tests two alternatives: a softplus hinge
(non-zero gradient everywhere) and a radius-projection post-step clamp.
The softplus ablation confirms that smoothing the hinge does not improve
stability, while projection and constrained parameterisation do---the
bottleneck is gradient magnitude, not relu non-differentiability.

Key findings: at lr = 0.1, 9/10 unregularised runs overshoot the unit
circle within 300 epochs.  Hinge regularisers provide minimal benefit:
spectral and contour achieve the same 1/10 stable rate as the
unregularised baseline; the Lyapunov penalty recovers one additional
seed (2/10 stable).  The constrained\_schur baseline achieves 10/10
stability and 10/10 certification, confirming that architectural hard
constraints are the reliable solution.  Stable runs under hinge methods
also certify (each of the 1--2 stable seeds passes the post-hoc
certificate), but are 8--9 seeds fewer.
\emph{Conclusion:} soft hinge regularisers are complementary to, not a
substitute for, hard constrained parameterisation under adversarial
gradient pressure.

\subsection{Experiment 3b: Softplus and radius-projection ablation}
\label{sec:exp3b}

Experiment~3 showed that hinge regularisers fail despite being active at
$\varrho_{\rm init}=0.96>0.9$: the regulariser gradient is overwhelmed by
the task gradient and Adam momentum, so a single lr=0.1 step crosses the
unit circle.  Experiment~3b tests two alternatives to distinguish gradient
dominance from the relu dead-zone (which is only relevant below $\varrho=0.9$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Spectral softplus}: replaces the relu hinge with a softplus
  hinge $\mathrm{softplus}(\beta(\varrho - \tau))/\beta$ ($\beta=10$),
  giving non-zero gradient everywhere, decaying exponentially far below
  the threshold.
\item \emph{Radius projection}: no penalty at all; instead, after each
  Adam step, log-radii are clamped so $r_i \leq 0.98$ for every mode
  (post-step projection).  This is a hard-ish barrier: the projection
  fires \emph{every} step regardless of gradient.
\end{itemize}
All other settings match Experiment~3 (lr=0.1, init $\varrho=0.96$,
margin $\tau=0.1$, weight $\lambda=1.0$, $n=8$, 300 epochs, 10 seeds).

\paragraph{Key findings.}
Softplus provides no improvement over the relu baseline (both 1/10 stable),
confirming that gradient dominance---not relu non-differentiability---is the
primary failure mechanism: even with non-zero gradient everywhere, the softplus
penalty is overwhelmed by the task gradient amplified by lr~$=0.1$ and Adam
momentum.  Post-step radius projection (spectral\_proj) achieves
10/10 stability: the projection fires every step regardless of gradient
magnitude, enforcing $\varrho \leq 0.98$ unconditionally.  The projection
is not architecturally equivalent to constrained\_schur (it does not
guarantee stability during the forward pass itself), but achieves the
same empirical result in this regime.

The softplus ablation shows that the failure is \emph{not} primarily
non-differentiability of the relu hinge: when the optimiser step is large
enough to cross the stability boundary in one update, any gradient-based
penalty is overwhelmed regardless of smoothness.

%% ============================================================
\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Gradient reference optimisation (Experiment~1) achieves 100\%
certification on the target families (diagonal stable, trained SSMs)
with mean margins $+0.96$ and $+0.99$ respectively, substantially
tighter than eig-shrunk ($+0.43$, $+0.79$) and random-search ($-0.53$,
$-0.09$).  For non-diagonal families, gradient optimisation narrows the
margin gap without closing it: Jordan near-boundary improves from
$-1.86$ (scalar) to $-0.97$; nonnormal stable from $-11.07$ to
$-4.98$.

Regulariser comparison (Experiment~2) shows all methods achieve 100\%
post-hoc certification in the standard constrained regime.  With margin
$\tau=0.1$, spectral and Lyapunov penalties are inactive during training
(models naturally maintain $\varrho<0.9$) and produce results identical
to the unregularised baseline; task losses differ by $\leq 0.3\%$.  The
contour barrier shows a modest but consistent $\varrho$ reduction on AR(4)
tasks ($\Delta\varrho\approx0.03$) via resolvent-geometry penalties that
remain active even below the spectral-radius threshold.

Adversarial regime (Experiment~3) reveals the fundamental limitation of
hinge regularisers: at lr = 0.1 with unconstrained radii, 90\% of
unregularised runs escape the unit disk, and hinge regularisers reduce
this to at best 80\% failure (Lyapunov, 20\% stable) because the hinge
gradient, though active at $\varrho_{\rm init}=0.96>0.9$, is overwhelmed
by a single lr=0.1 Adam update.
Experiment~3b ablates a softplus hinge and a post-step radius-projection
clamp; the projection substantially improves stability by enforcing
$r_i \leq 0.98$ every step, while the softplus hinge provides similar
results to the relu baseline because its gradient at $\varrho=0.96$ is
negligible.  The sigmoid-constrained (constrained\_schur) baseline
achieves 100\% stability on all seeds, confirming that architectural
constraints---not regularisation weight or threshold tuning---are the
reliable solution.

%% ============================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Reference optimisation.}
Gradient optimisation improves mean resolvent margins by $2.2\times$
over eig-shrunk on diagonal matrices ($+0.96$ vs.\ $+0.43$) and raises
certification from 36\% to 100\% on trained SSMs vs.\ random-search.
For non-diagonal structures the diagonal reference class is insufficient
regardless of optimisation method; structured non-diagonal references
(e.g.\ Schur-form based) are a natural extension.

\paragraph{Contour barrier vs spectral/Lyapunov.}
All four regularisation regimes achieve 100\% certification on diagonal
SSM tasks in the benign regime (Table~\ref{tab:regularisers}).  With
margin $\tau=0.1$, spectral and Lyapunov penalties are inactive: models
naturally converge to $\varrho\approx0.74$--$0.82$, below the firing
threshold $\varrho=0.9$.  Task losses are indistinguishable.  The contour
barrier shows a modest but consistent $\varrho$ reduction on AR(4) tasks
($\Delta\varrho\approx0.03$), because it penalises resolvent-margin
shortfall rather than spectral radius and remains sensitive to the
boundary geometry even when $\varrho$ is comfortably below threshold.
This difference is modest; the primary differentiation between soft and
hard stability strategies appears in the adversarial regime (Experiment~3),
not in the benign regime.

\paragraph{Hinge regularisers under adversarial gradient pressure.}
Experiment~3 establishes that relu-based hinges cannot prevent
instability when learning rates are high enough that a single gradient
step overshoots the unit circle.  The failure mechanism is
\emph{gradient dominance}: at $\varrho_\mathrm{init}=0.96$ the hinge is
already active (threshold $\varrho=0.9 < 0.96$), but the regulariser
gradient is insufficient to counteract the task gradient amplified by
lr~$=0.1$ and Adam momentum.
Experiment~3b tests two targeted alternatives.  The softplus hinge
provides non-zero gradient everywhere, but its effectiveness is limited:
even with a non-zero gradient at $\varrho=0.96$, the penalty is
overwhelmed by the same large update that defeats the relu hinge.
Post-step radius projection (hard clamp $r_i \leq 0.98$ after every
step) is more effective: it enforces the constraint on every iteration
regardless of gradient magnitude, unlike a penalty that must outweigh
the task gradient.  The sigmoid-parameterised constrained\_schur model
avoids the problem entirely: $\varrho < 0.98$ is enforced by the forward
pass, not by gradient dynamics, and is therefore immune to learning-rate
or momentum choices.  This is analogous to architectural stability
constraints in unitary RNNs~\cite{arjovsky2016unitary} and
Lie-group-constrained networks~\cite{lezcano2019cheap}, which enforce
hard spectral bounds without any regularisation term.

\paragraph{Practitioner guide.}
Table~\ref{tab:practitioner} summarises the recommended stability mechanism
for common settings, based on the experimental findings.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Scope.}
The claims of this paper concern diagonal complex SSMs.  They do not
imply that contour barriers solve stability regularisation for dense,
non-normal, or input-dependent SSMs, nor that hard sigmoid constraints
are the right answer for non-diagonal architectures.  Extending any
of the results---reference optimisation, regulariser comparison, or the
adversarial regime analysis---to structured non-diagonal models is
non-trivial and is not addressed here.

The gradient-optimised reference is computed on the \emph{initial}
model and frozen; co-optimising $A_0$ jointly with $A$ during training
is a natural extension.
The diagonal-fast-path assumption ($A$ diagonal) is inherent to the
current diagonal complex SSM architecture; extension to structured
non-diagonal SSMs requires the full $O(Kn^2)$ SVD path.
The DLR extension (Appendix~\ref{app:dlr}) is validated only on
diagonal-plus-low-rank matrices; a systematic comparison on non-normal
and Jordan families is deferred to future work.
The Experiment~3 constrained\_schur baseline uses a sigmoid bound of
$0.98$; tighter or adaptive bounds are an open design choice.

%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central message is a separation between soft and hard stability
guarantees.  Gradient reference optimisation closes the certification gap
from the companion paper: 100\% certification on diagonal and trained-SSM families,
versus $\leq 36\%$ with random search.  Training-time contour barriers
preserve larger spectral radii than spectral or Lyapunov penalties, but
do not improve task loss or resolvent margin---their benefit is
domain-specific (long-memory tasks).  Under adversarial gradient pressure,
hinge penalties, though active at $\varrho_\mathrm{init}=0.96$, are
overwhelmed by large Adam updates; the softplus ablation
(Experiment~3b) confirms the primary bottleneck is gradient dominance,
not relu non-differentiability---the issue is that any gradient-based
penalty can be overwhelmed by a sufficiently large step.  Hard enforcement mechanisms---radius projection or constrained
parameterisation---are reliable under adversarial gradient pressure; soft
penalties are useful regularisers and diagnostics, but they do not by
themselves provide hard stability.  The sigmoid-constrained architecture
achieves 100\% stability on every seed, demonstrating that soft and hard
stability mechanisms occupy complementary roles and cannot substitute for
each other.

\section{Diagonal-plus-low-rank reference}
\label{app:dlr}

A diagonal $A_0$ cannot closely approximate matrices with significant
off-diagonal structure.  We extend the parameterisation to
\emph{diagonal-plus-low-rank} (DLR):
\[
  A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(d_0) + U V^H, \quad U, V \in \C^{n \times r},
\]
with the diagonal part reparameterised as before and $U, V$
initialised with small complex Gaussian noise (scale $10^{-2}$) so
the low-rank branch has nonzero first-order gradients while $A_0$
remains close to the diagonal warm start.  Stability of $A_0$
is enforced via a softplus penalty on the spectral radius:
\[
  \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{stab}} = \mathrm{softplus}(\varrho(A_0) - (1 - \varepsilon)).
\]
The resolvent margin is computed via batched
\texttt{torch.linalg.solve} + SVD ($O(Kn^3)$ per step), since $A_0$
is no longer diagonal.  Rank $r = 1$ adds $4n$ real parameters beyond
the diagonal.

\paragraph{Scope and known limitations.}
Unit tests confirm that the DLR optimiser (i) returns a Schur-stable
$A_0$ and (ii) achieves higher resolvent margins than a scalar reference
on diagonal-plus-low-rank matrices ($n=6$, rank-1 perturbation).
Table~\ref{tab:refquality} shows that no diagonal-reference method
certifies the \texttt{nonnormal\_stable} or \texttt{jordan\_near\_boundary}
families; whether DLR closes this gap is an open question.  DLR
convergence is slower than the diagonal path due to the non-convex
stability penalty; we leave systematic evaluation to future work.
