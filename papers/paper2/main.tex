\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Thesis.}
Reference optimisation resolves the main post-hoc certification
bottleneck for diagonal SSMs; contour barriers are useful soft
margin-preserving training penalties; but only constrained
parameterisation provides hard stability guarantees under extreme
gradient pressure.

Post-hoc Rouch\'e certificates introduced in Paper~1 achieve high
false-negative rates with naive references.  We develop a gradient-based
algorithm that optimises the diagonal reference $A_0$ to maximise the
sampled resolvent margin, achieving 100\% certification on diagonal and
trained-SSM families (up from $\leq 36\%$ with random search).  Diagonal
references remain insufficient for non-normal and Jordan-near-boundary
matrices regardless of optimisation quality; these structures motivate a
diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR) extension presented here as an exploratory
baseline.

We embed the optimised reference into three training-time stability
regularisers---spectral penalty, Lyapunov per-mode penalty, and a
differentiable contour barrier---and compare them on autoregressive tasks.
In the standard constrained regime all three achieve 100\% post-hoc
certification; they differ only in the task-loss--spectral-radius
trade-off.  The contour barrier's distinctive property is
spectral-radius preservation ($\varrho\approx 0.68$ vs.\ $0.46$--$0.50$
for spectral/Lyapunov), \emph{not} superior task loss or resolvent margin.

In an adversarial unconstrained regime (lr=0.1, near-boundary init
$\varrho=0.96$), 90\% of unregularised runs overshoot the unit circle.
Hinge regularisers reduce but cannot eliminate instability (10--20\%
stable): a single Adam step at lr=0.1 can carry eigenvalues past the
unit circle before any relu gradient fires.  A sigmoid-parameterised
constrained trainer (spectral radius $< 0.98$ by construction) achieves
100\% stability on every seed, confirming that soft penalties are
complementary to---not a replacement for---hard architectural constraints.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Paper~1 established a framework of Rouch\'e-type contour certificates
for Schur stability of learned transition matrices.  The central
empirical finding was that both determinant and resolvent certificates
achieve less than 3\% certification rates when paired with scalar or
random-diagonal reference matrices, even for matrices the certificates
could in principle certify.  The bottleneck is the \emph{reference
matrix} $A_0$: a poorly chosen reference inflates the perturbation
$\norm{A_0 - A}$ relative to the resolvent margin, rendering the
certificate vacuous.

This paper makes four contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Gradient diagonal reference optimisation.}  We introduce
  an algorithm that directly maximises the sampled minimum resolvent
  margin $\hat{m}(A, A_0)$ w.r.t.\ diagonal $A_0$ via PyTorch autograd
  through a differentiable softmin.  For diagonal $A_0$ and diagonal $A$
  the margin reduces to $O(Kn)$ elementwise arithmetic; for general $A$
  we use the same fast diagonal path for $A_0$ with an $O(Kn^2)$ SVD
  for the operator norm.
\item \textbf{DLR reference extension (exploratory).}  We extend the
  parameterisation to diagonal-plus-low-rank (DLR) references for
  non-normal matrices.  The DLR path finds positive margins on controlled
  examples but is slower and its advantage over diagonal references on
  nonnormal/Jordan families is not yet established empirically;
  full validation is deferred to future work.
\item \textbf{Regulariser comparison.}  We embed the optimised reference
  into three training-time regularisers (spectral, Lyapunov, contour
  barrier) and compare them on AR tasks.  The contour barrier preserves
  larger spectral radii than spectral/Lyapunov penalties; it does
  \emph{not} achieve lower task loss or higher resolvent margins.
\item \textbf{Adversarial regime and constrained-parameterisation
  baseline.}  We show that under high learning rates and near-boundary
  initialisations, relu-based hinge regularisers cannot prevent unit-disk
  escape, while a sigmoid-parameterised model---where $\varrho < 0.98$
  is enforced by construction---achieves 100\% stability on every seed.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to Paper~1.}
All certificate definitions, Theorems 1--4, and the Paper~1 codebase
(\texttt{src/roche/}) remain unchanged.  This paper adds
\texttt{src/roche/reference\_opt.py} (gradient reference optimisation),
\texttt{src/roche/regularisers.py} (three regulariser implementations),
and an Adam-based SSM trainer (\texttt{train\_diagonal\_ssm\_adam}) to
\texttt{src/roche/ssm.py}.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Contour certificates (recap)}

Let $A \in \C^{n \times n}$ and $A_0 \in \C^{n \times n}$ be Schur stable.
The \emph{resolvent margin} at $z = e^{i\theta}$ is
\[
  m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, z)
  = 1 - \norm{(zI - A_0)^{-1}(A_0 - A)}_2.
\]
When $\min_\theta m_{\mathrm{res}} > 0$, Rouch\'e's theorem guarantees
$\varrho(A) < 1$.  The finite-grid certificate (Theorem~3 of Paper~1)
converts a sampled positive minimum margin into a continuum guarantee
via a Lipschitz criterion.

\subsection{The reference selection problem}

Define the \emph{optimal diagonal reference} as
\[
  A_0^* = \argmax_{A_0 \in \mathcal{D}} \min_{\theta} m_{\mathrm{res}}(A, A_0, \theta),
\]
where $\mathcal{D}$ is the set of diagonal Schur-stable matrices.
Paper~1's random search over 50--200 diagonal candidates achieves
near-zero certification rates for normal and non-normal families,
motivating a gradient-based approach.

\subsection{Diagonal fast path}

For $A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_n)$ with $A$ arbitrary,
\[
  (zI - A_0)^{-1}(A_0 - A) = \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{z - d_i}\right)(A_0 - A),
\]
i.e.\ each row $i$ of $A_0 - A$ is scaled by $1/(z - d_i)$.  The
2-norm of this product equals the largest singular value of a
row-scaled matrix, which costs $O(Kn^2)$ in general.  When $A$ is
\emph{also diagonal} (as in diagonal complex SSMs), $A_0 - A$ is
diagonal and the 2-norm reduces to $\max_i \abs{(d_{0,i} -
a_i)/(z - d_{0,i})}$, costing only $O(Kn)$ with no SVD.

\section{Gradient Reference Optimisation}
\label{sec:refopt}

\subsection{Parameterisation}

We parameterise $A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(r_i e^{i\phi_i})$ where
\[
  r_i = \sigma(\ell_i)(1 - \varepsilon), \quad \varepsilon = 0.02,
\]
with $\sigma$ the logistic function and $\ell_i, \phi_i \in \R$ the
free parameters.  This ensures $r_i \in (0, 1 - \varepsilon)$
without any projection step.

\subsection{Objective}

We maximise a differentiable lower bound on $\min_k m_{\mathrm{res}}(z_k)$
over a grid of $K$ equispaced contour points via a softmin:
\[
  \hat{m}_\beta(A, A_0)
  = -\frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_k \exp(-\beta \cdot m_{\mathrm{res}}(z_k)),
  \quad \beta = 20.
\]
Gradient ascent on $\hat{m}_\beta$ with respect to $(\ell_i, \phi_i)$
is performed with Adam (lr = 0.05, 300 steps) using PyTorch autograd.
Initialisation uses the eig-shrunk reference (Paper~1), which provides
a warm start with non-trivial margin for trained SSMs.

\subsection{Diagonal-plus-low-rank reference (extension)}
\label{sec:dlr}

A diagonal $A_0$ cannot closely approximate matrices with significant
off-diagonal structure.  We extend the parameterisation to
\emph{diagonal-plus-low-rank} (DLR):
\[
  A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(d_0) + U V^H, \quad U, V \in \C^{n \times r},
\]
with the diagonal part reparameterised as before and $U, V$
initialised to zero (so $A_0$ starts diagonal).  Stability of $A_0$
is enforced via a softplus penalty on the spectral radius of $A_0$
rather than by construction:
\[
  \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{stab}} = \mathrm{softplus}(\varrho(A_0) - (1 - \varepsilon)).
\]
The resolvent margin is computed via batched
$\texttt{torch.linalg.solve}$ + SVD ($O(Kn^3)$ per step), since $A_0$
is no longer diagonal.  Rank $r = 1$ adds $4n$ real parameters beyond
the diagonal.

\paragraph{Current scope.}
Unit tests confirm that the DLR optimiser (i) returns a Schur-stable $A_0$
and (ii) achieves higher resolvent margins than a scalar reference on
diagonal-plus-low-rank matrices (\texttt{diagonal\_plus\_low\_rank} family
with rank-1 perturbation, $n=6$).  A systematic experiment comparing DLR
to diagonal references across all four families from
Table~\ref{tab:refquality} is deferred to future work.  DLR convergence is
slower than the diagonal path due to the non-convex stability penalty and
the larger parameter space; whether the added expressivity yields
certification on non-normal or Jordan families is an open question.

\section{Stability Regularisers}
\label{sec:regularisers}

All three regularisers take the diagonal eigenvalue vector
$\mathbf{a} = (a_1, \ldots, a_n) \in \C^n$ and return a real scalar.

\paragraph{Spectral penalty.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{spec}}(\mathbf{a}) = \mathrm{relu}\!\left(\max_i \abs{a_i} - (1 - \tau)\right),
\]
where $\tau$ is a stability margin.  This is a hinge on the spectral
radius; only the largest mode is penalised.  \texttt{relu} rather than
\texttt{softplus} is used so the penalty is \emph{exactly zero} for
clearly stable systems, avoiding a constant background term.

\paragraph{Lyapunov penalty.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{Lyap}}(\mathbf{a}) = \sum_i \mathrm{relu}\!\left(\abs{a_i}^2 - (1 - \tau)\right).
\]
For diagonal $A$ with $P = I$ the Lyapunov condition $A^\top P A - P \prec 0$
reduces to $\abs{a_i}^2 < 1$ per mode.  The sum formulation penalises
\emph{all} near-unstable modes jointly, yielding different gradient dynamics
from the spectral hinge.

\paragraph{Contour barrier.}
\[
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{ctour}}(\mathbf{a}) = \mathrm{relu}\!\left(-\hat{m}_\beta(A, A_0^*) + \tau\right),
\]
where $A_0^*$ is the gradient-optimised reference computed once from
the initial model and frozen throughout training.  The barrier fires
only when the resolvent margin falls below $\tau$, providing a
certificate-geometry-aware signal.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Adversarial unconstrained regime}
\label{sec:exp3}

To isolate whether hinge regularisers can \emph{prevent} instability
(rather than merely coexist with it in an already-stable regime), we
train with \emph{unconstrained} eigenvalue radii, a high learning rate
(lr = 0.1), adversarial near-boundary initialisation ($\varrho_{\mathrm{init}} = 0.96$),
and a long-memory AR(4) task ($\varrho_{\max} \approx 0.97$, seq.\ length 2000).
Regulariser threshold is $\tau = 0.1$ (fires at $\varrho = 0.9$) with weight
$\lambda = 1.0$.  Ten seeds per method; $n = 8$, 300 epochs.

\paragraph{Constrained-parameterisation baseline.}
As a hard-stability reference we include a fifth method,
\emph{constrained\_schur}, which replaces the exponential radius
parameterisation with $r_i = \sigma(\ell_i)(1-\varepsilon)$
($\varepsilon = 0.02$; $\sigma$ the logistic function).  This enforces
$\varrho(A) < 0.98$ by construction: no regulariser, no projection---the
architecture itself is the constraint.  With the same high learning rate
and near-boundary initialisation, the sigmoid parameterisation cannot
overshoot the unit circle regardless of gradient magnitude.

\paragraph{Failure mechanism.}
At lr = 0.1, Adam momentum can carry eigenvalue radii by
$\Delta\varrho \approx 0.15$ per step.
Starting from $\varrho_0 = 0.96$, a single step suffices to cross
$\varrho = 1$.  The relu-based hinge has \emph{exactly zero gradient}
below its threshold ($\tau = 0.1$, firing at $\varrho = 0.9$); the
gradient activates only at $\varrho \geq 0.9$, but the unit-circle
crossing occurs in one step from $\varrho = 0.96$ with no intermediate
evaluation.  A softplus hinge would provide non-zero gradient everywhere
but introduces a constant background penalty across all modes; we leave
this ablation to future work.  The fundamental bottleneck is not the
hinge form but the step-size: gradient clipping, adaptive per-mode lr,
or constrained parameterisation all resolve it.

Key findings: at lr = 0.1, 90\% of unregularised runs overshoot the
unit circle within 300 epochs.  Hinge regularisers provide minimal
benefit: spectral and contour achieve the same 10\% stability rate as
the unregularised baseline; the Lyapunov penalty recovers one additional
seed (20\% stable).  The constrained\_schur baseline achieves 100\%
stability across all 10 seeds and certifies 100\% of runs (eig-shrunk
reference suffices for sigmoid-parameterised diagonal SSMs), confirming
that architectural hard constraints are the reliable solution.
Stable runs under hinge methods also certify (100\%), but are 80--90\%
fewer in number.
\emph{Conclusion:} soft hinge regularisers are complementary to, not a
substitute for, hard constrained parameterisation under adversarial
gradient pressure.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Gradient reference optimisation (Experiment~1) achieves 100\%
certification on the target families (diagonal stable, trained SSMs)
with mean margins $+0.96$ and $+0.99$ respectively, substantially
tighter than eig-shrunk ($+0.43$, $+0.79$) and random-search ($-0.53$,
$-0.09$).  For non-diagonal families, gradient optimisation narrows the
margin gap without closing it: Jordan near-boundary improves from
$-1.86$ (scalar) to $-0.97$; nonnormal stable from $-11.07$ to
$-4.98$.

Regulariser comparison (Experiment~2) shows all methods achieve 100\%
post-hoc certification in the standard constrained regime.  The spectral
penalty achieves the lowest task loss ($\leq 6\%$ overhead), the Lyapunov
penalty incurs up to 24\% overhead but achieves the highest resolvent margin
($+0.92$), and the contour barrier provides the best spectral-radius
preservation ($\varrho \approx 0.68$) by not penalising stable modes beyond
the certificate threshold.

Adversarial regime (Experiment~3) reveals the fundamental limitation of
hinge regularisers: at lr = 0.1 with unconstrained radii, 90\% of
unregularised runs escape the unit disk, and hinge regularisers reduce
this to at best 80\% failure (Lyapunov, 20\% stable) because a single
Adam step overshoots the unit circle before the relu gradient fires.
The sigmoid-constrained (constrained\_schur) baseline achieves 100\%
stability on all seeds, confirming that architectural constraints---not
regularisation weight or threshold tuning---are the reliable solution.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Reference optimisation.}
Gradient optimisation improves mean resolvent margins by $2.2\times$
over eig-shrunk on diagonal matrices ($+0.96$ vs.\ $+0.43$) and raises
certification from 36\% to 100\% on trained SSMs vs.\ random-search.
For non-diagonal structures the diagonal reference class is insufficient
regardless of optimisation method; structured non-diagonal references
(e.g.\ Schur-form based) are a natural extension.

\paragraph{Contour barrier vs spectral/Lyapunov.}
All three regularisers achieve 100\% certification on diagonal SSM tasks
in the benign regime, matching the unregularised baseline.  The spectral
penalty achieves the lowest task-loss overhead ($\leq 6\%$).  The
Lyapunov penalty achieves the highest resolvent margin ($+0.92$) but
incurs up to 24\% task-loss increase.  The contour barrier does
\emph{not} outperform spectral regularisation on task loss or resolvent
margin; its sole advantage is spectral-radius preservation
($\varrho \approx 0.68$ vs.\ $0.46$--$0.50$ for spectral/Lyapunov).
This preservation matters for long-memory tasks where near-boundary modes
carry predictive signal; it is irrelevant when memory is short.

\paragraph{Hinge regularisers under adversarial gradient pressure.}
Experiment~3 establishes that relu-based hinges cannot prevent
instability when learning rates are high enough that a single gradient
step overshoots the unit circle.  The failure mechanism is the
\emph{dead-zone} of relu: gradient is exactly zero below the hinge
threshold, and the threshold ($\varrho = 0.9$) lies 0.06 below the init
($\varrho = 0.96$), well within a single lr = 0.1 Adam step.  Softplus
hinges would mitigate the dead-zone but introduce a constant background
penalty.  The sigmoid-parameterised constrained\_schur model avoids the
problem entirely: $\varrho < 0.98$ is enforced by the forward pass, not
by gradient dynamics, and is therefore immune to learning-rate or
momentum choices.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The gradient-optimised reference is computed on the \emph{initial}
model and frozen; co-optimising $A_0$ jointly with $A$ during training
is a natural extension.
The diagonal-fast-path assumption ($A$ diagonal) is inherent to the
current diagonal complex SSM architecture; extension to structured
non-diagonal SSMs requires the full $O(Kn^2)$ SVD path.
The DLR extension (Section~\ref{sec:dlr}) is validated only on
diagonal-plus-low-rank matrices; a systematic comparison on non-normal
and Jordan families is deferred to future work.
The Experiment~3 constrained\_schur baseline uses a sigmoid bound of
$0.98$; tighter or adaptive bounds are an open design choice.

%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central message is a separation between soft and hard stability
guarantees.  Gradient reference optimisation closes the certification gap
from Paper~1: 100\% certification on diagonal and trained-SSM families,
versus $\leq 36\%$ with random search.  Training-time contour barriers
preserve larger spectral radii than spectral or Lyapunov penalties, but
do not improve task loss or resolvent margin---their benefit is
domain-specific (long-memory tasks).  Under adversarial gradient pressure,
all hinge regularisers fail to prevent unit-disk escape because the
relu dead-zone admits a single overshoot step; the sigmoid-constrained
architecture achieves 100\% stability on every seed, demonstrating that
hard constraints and soft regularisers occupy complementary roles and
cannot substitute for each other.
